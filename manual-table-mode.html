Part 2

Building the setup sheet

Table Mode is where you spend most of a session: typing in sources, choosing mics and preamps, and numbering channels and tie lines. This part covers everything on that screen, starting with what you'll use every time.

The header

The strip across the top of the editor holds the setup's details. Click any of them to edit it in place.

  • Setup name. The large box on the left. It becomes the title of the PDF and the suggested file name when you export.
  • Session date. Click the calendar icon to pick a date, or type one. New setups start with today's date.
  • Engineer and Artist. Both are optional. They print on the line under the title.

If you fill in the same engineer name every time, set Default engineer name in Settings → General. Every new setup then starts with it filled in.

Session notes

The button with the page icon, just right of the Artist box, opens Session notes. Use it for anything that applies to the whole session rather than one source: a tuning reference, mic-array spacing, a reminder about the talkback. Click anywhere outside the box to close it.

When a setup has notes, the icon on the button turns the accent color, so you can tell at a glance that there's something to read.

Adding sources

Each row of the sheet is one source: a mic, a DI, a line input. There are three ways to add one.

  • Type and press Return. Click the Add a source box above the sheet (it stays in view as you scroll), type a name such as Lead Vocal, and press Return (or click Add source). The box clears and stays ready, so you can type a whole input list in one go.
  • Press ⌘N. This adds an empty row that you can name afterward.
  • Double-click blank space below the table, or the message on an empty sheet. This also adds an empty row.

New rows go to the bottom of the active band. On a sheet without bands, that's the bottom of the sheet. The active band explains how to pick one. To rename a source, click its Source name cell and type.

Choosing gear

The Mic, Preamp and Outboard cells each open a picker. Click the cell to open it.

Where gear comes from

Gear is grouped by where it lives, always in this order. You only see the groups that have something in them.

  1. This Studio: the room's own gear list.
  2. This Session: gear borrowed for this setup only, added in Setup settings → Session Gear.
  3. Building Office: shared gear for rooms in the 150 building.
  4. Personal Gear Locker: your own gear, from Settings → Personal Gear Locker. It's offered in every studio.
  5. Faculty Reserve: Berklee's faculty pool, when the setup has it switched on.

Inside each group, gear is listed by manufacturer. Point at a manufacturer to see its models, sorted by model number, then click one. A manufacturer's name is matched without regard to capitals, so dbx and DBX share one submenu.

Finding a model quickly

  • Search. Start typing in the Search… box at the top of the picker. Results replace the browsing list. If the same model is stocked in more than one place, it appears once with the combined count, and the studio's own copies are used first.
  • Type. With a Mic, Preamp or Outboard cell focused but closed, type a letter or number. The picker opens with that character in the Search… box.
  • Keyboard. Use ↑ and ↓ to move, → to open a group, ← to back out, and Return to pick.
  • No Mic (or No Preamp, No Outboard) empties the cell.
  • Custom… lets you type any name, for gear that isn't on any list. Enter it under Gear name and click Save. A custom name isn't counted against anything.

How double-booking is prevented

Every model in the picker shows a count such as 2/5 in use: two are already on other rows, out of five the room has. When all of them are taken, the model is grayed out and can't be picked. You can't put the same mic on more rows than there are mics.

A few units are both a preamp and a processor, such as the UA 6176 or the Millennia STT-1. They appear in both the Preamp and Outboard pickers, but they share one count, so two 6176s can't turn into four. On a single row, a unit you've picked as the preamp isn't offered again as outboard. Patching it once already gives you both halves.

Automatic notes tags

When you pick gear from Building Office, Faculty Reserve or Personal Gear Locker, the app adds a tag such as [Faculty Reserve] to the end of that row's Notes. It reminds whoever sets up the room that the item has to be fetched. Anything you typed in Notes is left alone, and the tag changes or disappears if you pick something else.

The other cells

48V
Tick for phantom power. It prints as ✓.
Channel
The console channel, as a whole number from 1 up.
Tie line
The tie line or wall-box input. It takes a number, or a label such as PB1 if that's how your patch bay names its lines. Two rows with the same tie line get a warning (see Warnings).
Cue box
Free text, so a stereo cue like 1-2 works as well as a single number.
Polarity
Tick to flip polarity. It prints as Ø.
Notes
Anything else about this source: placement, a pad, a reminder.
Outboard
Starts as one column. Use the − and + buttons next to the column count in the toolbar to add more outboard columns, one per piece of gear in the chain. Removing a column takes away the last one and clears it on every row. There's always at least one.

To hide columns you don't use or change their order, open Columns in the toolbar. Part 8 covers it, along with stereo pairs: the small link icons between rows join two rows into a pair that stays in step.

Quick setups

A quick setup (from Start a quick setup → on the home screen) has no studio behind it, so there's no gear list to pick from. Its Mic, Outboard and Preamp cells are plain text boxes instead, with the placeholders Mic, Outboard and Preamp.

  • Start typing and a list of matching gear appears, drawn from every studio and gear list in the app. It shows up to 20 matches.
  • Click a suggestion, or use ↑ and ↓ and press Return. The list shows the manufacturer to help you find the right one, but only the model name goes in the cell.
  • You don't have to pick a suggestion. Whatever you type stays exactly as typed. Press Esc to close the list.

Because there's no stock to count, a quick setup shows no "in use" counts and doesn't stop you from using a name twice.

Selecting rows

Selecting rows lets you number, color, move, duplicate or delete several at once. Each row has a gutter at its left edge, where the grip handle is. Click the gutter, not the cells, to select.

  • Click a row's gutter to select that row.
  • ⇧-click another row to select everything in between.
  • ⌘-click to add or remove one row without touching the rest.
  • ⌘A selects every row (when you're not typing in a box).
  • Esc clears the selection.

Selected rows get a blue bar along the left edge of the sheet, and the selection bar appears above the table.

Power user

You can select without the mouse. Press Tab until a row's gutter has focus, then use ↑ and ↓ to move between rows and ⇧↑ or ⇧↓ to extend the selection. Space or Return selects the focused row.

The selection bar

The selection bar appears whenever rows are selected. It says how many, such as 4 rows selected, and everything on it acts on those rows only.

Number

Number fills in a run of channels, tie lines or cue boxes for you.

  1. Select the rows to number.
  2. Next to Number, choose Channel, Tie line or Cue box.
  3. In the from box, type the first number.
  4. Click Apply, or press Return in the number box.

The selected rows are numbered from top to bottom: 17, 18, 19 and so on. Anything already in that column on those rows is replaced.

Tip

Press ⌘⇧N with rows selected to jump straight to the number box. Type the start number and press Return.

Color, Save as preset, Delete

  • Color opens a palette of swatches. Pick one to tint every selected row, which helps group drums, keys and vocals at a glance. No color removes the tint. Row colors also appear in the PDF (when Colored is on) and in spreadsheets.
  • Save as preset saves the selected rows as a channel preset you can load into other setups. See Part 8.
  • Delete deletes the selected rows.
  • The × at the right end clears the selection.

Moving, duplicating and deleting

  • Move. Drag a row by its grip handle. If you drag one of several selected rows, the whole selection moves together. Dragging either row of a stereo pair moves both. A row dropped between the two halves of a pair lands past the pair: below it when you drag down, above it when you drag up.
  • Duplicate. Press ⌘D to copy the selected rows. Each block of selected rows is copied right below itself, and the copies become the new selection. A copy never lands between the two rows of a stereo pair. A pair copied whole becomes a new pair, and a copy of one half goes below the pair, unlinked.
  • Delete. Click the red × at the end of a row, click Delete in the selection bar, or press ⌘⌫. The forward-delete key (fn⌫ on a laptop) works too, without ⌘.

After a delete, a message such as Deleted 1 row appears at the bottom of the window for five seconds. Click Undo on it to bring the rows back.

Bands

A setup sheet can hold several bands, played one after another in the same session. Each band starts with a divider row, and its rows run below it until the next divider.

The divider row

The divider has three boxes for the band's name, its start time and its notes. Type in any of them. The name and start time head the band in the PDF and in the spreadsheet (see Bands in exports).

  • The fold arrow hides the band's rows and leaves the divider with a count, such as 6 rows. Click it again to show the rows.
  • The ⋯ button opens the band's options.
OptionWhat it does
Show only this bandShows this band alone. A banner above the table reads Showing only with the band's name, and its Show all bands button brings the others back.
Changeover…Opens the changeover panel for this band. Shown on every band except the first.
Merge into previous bandRemoves this divider, so its rows join the band before. Shown on every band except the first.
Remove dividerRemoves the divider from a sheet with one band. The rows stay. Shown when the sheet has one band.
Remove band…Removes the band and all its rows after you confirm. You can undo it right after. Shown when the sheet has two or more bands.

The active band

The active band is the one new rows go into. Its divider has an accent bar along the left edge. Click the bar, outside its buttons, to make that band active. Clicking into one of its boxes does the same.

New rows, and the rows of a loaded channel preset, go to the bottom of the active band. Until you pick a band, they go to the band you're showing alone, or else to the last band.

The bands menu

Click the stack icon in the Table Mode toolbar to open the bands menu. It lists every band in order with its start time. A check mark shows the active band.

  • Pick a band. Click its name. That band becomes active, and the table scrolls to its divider.
  • Reorder. Drag a band by its grip handle. Its divider and rows move together, every band unfolds, and the change marks are worked out again for the new order.
  • Show changes between bands. A switch that appears when the sheet has two or more bands. It turns the change marks in the table on or off. The exports have their own setting.
  • Show all bands. Appears while the table shows one band. Click it to show every band again.
  • Add band. Opens the choice described below.

Adding a band

Choose Add band in the bands menu. Then choose where the new band starts.

  • Copy of a band. The new band gets a copy of every row in that band, with the same gear, channels, tie lines and cue boxes. The choices list the bands from last to first. A sheet without bands offers Copy of this setup instead.
  • Blank band. The new band starts with no rows.

The new divider goes after the last band and becomes the active band, and the table scrolls to it. Its name is Band 2, Band 3 and so on until you rename it. A sheet without bands first gets a divider named Band 1 above its existing rows.

Shortcut

⌘B opens the bands menu on its add step. You can change the key under Edit in Settings → Keybinds. It works in Table Mode.

Changes between bands

Each band after the first is compared with the band before it. A row is matched to the row it was copied from, so a rename or a move keeps the match.

With Show changes between bands on, which is the default, the table marks what changed:

  • A changed cell has an amber tint and an amber line under it. Its old value shows under the new one, struck through. The marks cover the Source name, Mic, 48V, Outboard, Channel, Preamp, Tie line, Cue box and Polarity columns.
  • A new row has a + before its source name. It has no match in the band before.
  • Hover a marked cell to read the whole row's change, such as Fiddle (ch 3): mic KM184 → SM81; 48V off → on.

A band's divider shows a button such as 4 changes from Early set, 1 removed. It counts the rows with a patch change, the new rows and the removed rows. Removed rows are listed here and in the changeover panel.

The changeover panel

The panel lists what to change between the band before and this one. Open it with Changeover… in a band's ⋯ menu, or click the change count on its divider.

The panel is titled Changeover to and the band's name. It has three sections: Re-patch, Add and Remove. Only sections with entries appear. A band with the same patch as the band before shows Same patch as the band before.

Click Print… to save the changeover as a one-page PDF. If the sheet has unsaved changes, the app saves it first. If that save fails, the panel shows Couldn't save the sheet, so nothing was printed. Otherwise, choose where to save the file. Close the panel with Close or Esc.

Warnings

Two kinds of yellow warning can appear inside a cell.

duplicate tie line
Two or more rows share the same tie line. Capitals and spaces don't count, so pb1 and PB1 clash. The warning shows on every row involved. Change one of them and it goes away. If you export anyway, the PDF puts a "!" in front of those tie lines so the clash is easy to spot on paper.
Preset expected: …
You loaded a channel preset that names gear this studio doesn't have, such as Preset expected: U 47 FET. The row is added with that cell left empty. Pick something from the picker and the warning clears. On a linked pair, gear copied to the other row clears its warning too.

Undo and saving

Undo and redo

Press ⌘Z to undo and ⌘⇧Z to redo. The app keeps your last 100 steps. Undo covers rows and everything in them, the number of outboard columns, the header fields and session notes.

  • Column choices (which columns are shown, and their order) aren't part of undo. Change them back in the Columns menu.
  • While you're typing in a box, ⌘Z undoes your typing in that box first.

Autosave

There's no Save button to remember. The app saves about a second after you stop typing, and at least every five seconds if you keep going. It also saves when you leave the setup or quit. Press ⌘S any time to save right away.

The save status sits in the toolbar, just right of the header fields:

  • Unsaved changes: you've made a change that hasn't been written yet.
  • Saving…: it's being written now.
  • Saved, with a check mark: done. It shows for a couple of seconds, then disappears.
  • Couldn't save — retrying, in red: something went wrong. Keep working. The app tries again every 10 seconds and saves as soon as it can.

Each setup also remembers which mode you left it in, so it reopens in Table Mode or Layout Mode just as you left it.
